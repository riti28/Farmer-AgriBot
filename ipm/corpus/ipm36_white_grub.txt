Title: White grubs in soil
URL: https://www.uasd.edu/
Org: University of Agricultural Sciences (UAS), Dharwad

White grubs are the C-shaped, creamy white larvae of beetles (chafer beetles). They live in the soil and eat roots of sugarcane, groundnut, maize, arecanut and many other crops, so plants turn yellow, wilt and can be pulled out easily. Adult beetles come out of the soil after the first good summer or pre-monsoon showers and gather on nearby trees like neem and acacia at night to feed and mate. Collecting and destroying beetles from these trees for a few evenings after the first rains, as a community, greatly reduces the next generation. Deep ploughing exposes grubs to birds. Light traps catch adult beetles. Soil application of entomopathogenic fungi or nematodes, or a recommended insecticide, is used in fields with a history of white grub.
