Title: Early shoot borer in sugarcane
URL: https://sugarcane.icar.gov.in/
Org: ICAR-Sugarcane Breeding Institute (SBI)

Early shoot borer attacks sugarcane in the first three to four months after planting, from germination to tillering. The larva enters the young shoot near the ground and kills the growing point, producing a dead heart that smells bad and pulls out easily. Hot, dry weather with low humidity, typical of summer months before the monsoon, favours this pest. Loss of shoots reduces the number of millable canes. Light and frequent irrigation, which raises humidity near the plants, reduces attack. Monitoring involves counting dead hearts in sample rows; around 15% dead hearts is often taken as the action level.
