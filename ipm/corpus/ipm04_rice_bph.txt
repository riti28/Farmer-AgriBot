Title: Brown planthopper in rice: signs and management
URL: https://www.icar-iirr.org/
Org: ICAR-Indian Institute of Rice Research (IIRR)

Brown planthopper (BPH) is a small brown insect that sits at the base of rice plants near the water line and sucks sap. Heavy feeding causes round patches of dried, yellow-brown plants called hopper burn. Warm and humid weather, dense planting and too much nitrogen fertiliser favour BPH build-up, mostly from tillering to the grain filling stage. IPM steps: grow tolerant varieties where available, keep 20 cm alleys every 2-3 metres so air and light reach the base, split nitrogen doses, and drain the field for a few days when hoppers increase. Spiders, mirid bugs and water striders eat hoppers, so avoid early broad-spectrum sprays. A commonly used action level is about 10 hoppers per hill; then use an insecticide recommended for BPH and direct the spray to the plant base. Some synthetic pyrethroids can cause BPH resurgence and should be avoided.
