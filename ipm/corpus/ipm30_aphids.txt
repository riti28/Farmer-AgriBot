Title: Aphids: small sap-sucking insects
URL: https://ipm.ucanr.edu/
Org: University of California Statewide IPM Program (UC IPM)

Aphids are tiny soft-bodied insects, green, black, yellow or grey, that live in groups on young shoots and under leaves. They suck sap, so leaves curl, plants become weak and growth slows. Aphids produce a sticky honeydew on which black sooty mould grows, and some aphids spread plant viruses. Their numbers rise quickly in cool, dry weather and fall sharply after heavy rain. Lady beetles, lacewing larvae, hoverfly larvae and tiny parasitic wasps eat aphids, so check for these helpers before spraying. Control aphids by removing heavily infested shoots, avoiding too much nitrogen fertiliser, using yellow sticky traps to monitor winged aphids, and spraying water, neem oil or soap solution on small colonies. Use a recommended insecticide only when colonies spread over many plants and natural enemies are few.
