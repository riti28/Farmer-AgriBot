Title: Whitefly in cotton and vegetables
URL: https://cicr.org.in/
Org: ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR)

Whiteflies are very small white winged insects that fly up in a cloud when an infested plant is shaken. Adults and the flat scale-like young stages live under the leaves and suck sap. Heavy attack causes yellowing, sticky honeydew, sooty mould and leaf drop, and whitefly spreads cotton leaf curl virus and many vegetable viruses. Hot, dry weather favours whitefly. IPM steps: remove weed hosts, avoid growing cotton next to other whitefly host crops, use yellow sticky traps, avoid excess nitrogen, and protect predators such as lacewings and lady beetles. Neem oil or neem seed kernel extract sprays on the underside of leaves help when numbers are low. Repeated sprays of synthetic pyrethroids can cause whitefly outbreaks and should be avoided.
