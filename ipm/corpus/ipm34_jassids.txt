Title: Jassids (leafhoppers) in cotton, okra and other crops
URL: https://cicr.org.in/
Org: ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR)

Jassids, also called leafhoppers, are small wedge-shaped pale green insects that walk sideways and jump when disturbed. Adults and nymphs suck sap from the underside of leaves. The leaf edges first turn yellow, then curl down and become reddish-brown and dry, a symptom called hopper burn. Jassids are common in the early vegetative stage of cotton and okra, especially in humid weather. Hairy-leaved cotton varieties suffer less. Seed treatment protects young plants for the first few weeks. Scout leaves on several plants; spray only when many leaves show yellowing edges and nymph numbers exceed the local action level.
