Title: Pink bollworm in cotton: biology and monitoring
URL: https://cicr.org.in/
Org: ICAR-Central Institute for Cotton Research (CICR)

Pink bollworm larvae feed inside flowers and bolls of cotton. Damaged flowers stay closed like a rosette, bolls rot or open badly and lint quality falls. Pink bollworm has developed resistance to Bt cotton toxins in India, so Bt alone no longer protects the crop. Moths are active mostly in the second half of the season, from flowering to boll maturity, and populations often peak in cooler months. Monitoring uses pheromone traps (gossyplure) placed at canopy height, about 5 per hectare. A commonly used action level is 8 moths per trap per night for 3 nights in a row, or 10% damaged flowers or green bolls. Farmers should open about 20 green bolls per acre each week to check for larvae.
